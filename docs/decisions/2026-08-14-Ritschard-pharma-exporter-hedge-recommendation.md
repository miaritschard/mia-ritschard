# FX Hedge Recommendation — U.S. Pharmaceutical Exporter

**To:** Chief Financial Officer
**From:** Amelia Ritschard
**Date:** August 14, 2026
**Subject:** Recommendation for EUR 8,000,000 Receivable Exposure

## Executive Summary

The company expects to receive EUR 8,000,000 in one year, creating a foreign-exchange exposure because the value of those euros in U.S. dollars will change with the EUR/USD exchange rate. The primary risk is depreciation of the euro against the U.S. dollar before settlement. Based on the current market inputs and sensitivity analysis, I recommend fully hedging the receivable with a **forward contract**.

At the current one-year forward rate of 1.1747 USD/EUR, the company can lock in **$9,397,600** of USD proceeds. This eliminates uncertainty surrounding the dollar value of the receivable, requires no upfront option premium, and provides a straightforward hedge that closely matches the amount and timing of the underlying exposure.

## A. Exposure Summary

The company has a one-year EUR 8,000,000 receivable. Because the company's functional cash-flow objective is measured in U.S. dollars, EUR depreciation would reduce the USD value ultimately received.

The current spot rate is 1.1535 USD/EUR. If the company remains unhedged and the spot rate is unchanged at settlement, the receivable would be worth:

`EUR 8,000,000 × 1.1535 USD/EUR = USD 9,228,000`

However, that amount is not guaranteed. The sensitivity analysis shows that changes in EUR/USD can materially alter the dollar proceeds.

## B. Hedge Outcomes

Five strategies were evaluated: remaining unhedged, using a forward contract, establishing a money-market hedge, purchasing a EUR put option, and purchasing a EUR call option.

### Unhedged Position

The unhedged strategy provides full participation in favorable EUR appreciation but leaves the company completely exposed to depreciation.

At a 5% decline in EUR/USD, the receivable would generate only **$8,766,600**. At the current spot rate, it would generate **$9,228,000**, while a 5% appreciation would increase proceeds to **$9,689,400**.

Although the upside is attractive, this strategy provides no protection against the company's primary FX risk.

### Forward Hedge

The forward hedge locks in the one-year forward rate of 1.1747 USD/EUR:

`EUR 8,000,000 × 1.1747 = USD 9,397,600`

The resulting **$9,397,600** is known today and does not change with the future spot rate. The forward therefore provides complete downside protection and maximum budget certainty, although the company gives up the opportunity to benefit from EUR appreciation beyond the contracted rate.

### Money-Market Hedge

The money-market hedge produces approximately **$9,397,325** at maturity. This outcome is essentially equivalent to the forward hedge and also provides a highly certain USD value.

The approximately $275 difference between the money-market and forward proceeds is attributable to rounding in the documented forward rate. Economically, the two strategies are nearly equivalent. However, the money-market hedge requires multiple transactions: borrowing euros, converting them to dollars at spot, and investing the dollar proceeds. The forward achieves essentially the same economic result with a simpler transaction structure.

### EUR Put Option

The EUR put protects against depreciation while preserving the ability to benefit if the euro appreciates. With a strike rate of 1.1535 USD/EUR and a premium of $0.0200 per euro, the total premium is **$160,000**.

If the euro depreciates below the strike, the put establishes net proceeds of **$9,068,000** after the premium. At the +5% sensitivity point, the put produces **$9,529,400**, allowing the company to participate in favorable appreciation.

The put therefore provides valuable flexibility, but the $160,000 premium reduces proceeds and represents a certain cost regardless of whether the option is ultimately exercised.

### EUR Call Option

The EUR call is useful as a comparative payoff strategy but is not an appropriate primary hedge for this exposure. Because the company already expects to receive euros, its principal risk is a decline in the value of the euro. The call does not provide protection against that depreciation and also requires a $160,000 premium.

For this reason, I would not recommend the EUR call for hedging this receivable.

## C. Sensitivity Interpretation

The sensitivity analysis highlights the tradeoff between **certainty and flexibility**.

If the euro depreciates by 5%, the unhedged position falls to $8,766,600. In contrast, the forward remains at $9,397,600 and the money-market hedge remains at approximately $9,397,325. The put provides partial protection at $9,068,000 after its premium.

At the unchanged spot-rate scenario, the unhedged position produces $9,228,000, while the forward and money-market hedge remain near $9.398 million. The put produces $9,068,000 because of the option premium.

If the euro appreciates by 5%, remaining unhedged produces $9,689,400 and the put produces $9,529,400. These strategies outperform the fixed forward proceeds under substantial EUR appreciation because they retain upside participation. However, the benefit comes with either full downside exposure in the unhedged case or the $160,000 premium in the put case.

The decision therefore depends on whether management places greater value on predictable cash flows or on retaining potential FX upside.

## D. Recommendation

I recommend that the company use the **forward hedge for the full EUR 8,000,000 receivable**.

The forward locks in **$9,397,600**, which is approximately $169,600 more than the USD value of the receivable at today's spot rate. More importantly, the amount is known in advance and does not depend on the future EUR/USD exchange rate.

The money-market hedge provides nearly identical protection and proceeds, but its additional borrowing, conversion, and investment steps create unnecessary operational complexity relative to the forward.

The put offers flexibility and upside participation, but management must pay $160,000 for that flexibility. Given that the objective of the hedge is to protect the USD value of a known foreign-currency receivable, the forward provides a stronger balance of protection, simplicity, and cost.

## E. Executive Justification

From a cash-flow-management perspective, the forward contract provides the strongest fit for this exposure. Locking in $9,397,600 allows management to budget around a known dollar inflow rather than expose operating cash flow to movements in EUR/USD.

The strategy also preserves liquidity relative to an option because there is no upfront $160,000 premium. Compared with the money-market hedge, it avoids the need to arrange a EUR borrowing and USD investment while delivering essentially the same maturity value.

The principal disadvantage is the loss of upside participation. If the euro appreciates substantially, the company will still receive only the contracted $9,397,600 rather than the higher amount available from remaining unhedged or using a put. However, accepting that opportunity cost is consistent with the purpose of hedging: reducing uncertainty rather than speculating on favorable exchange-rate movements.

For a known EUR 8,000,000 receivable with a defined one-year settlement horizon, the **forward contract is therefore the most appropriate strategy** based on the available market data. It provides complete downside protection, strong budget certainty, no upfront premium, and a simple structure that directly offsets the company's foreign-exchange exposure.
