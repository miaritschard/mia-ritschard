## Audit Purpose

I audited the generated FX hedging workbook against the Phase 2 technical specification and the Phase 3 build requirements. I focused on the named-range contract, formula integrity, hedge calculations, parity validation, and sensitivity analysis.

## Finding 1 — Named Ranges and Formula Structure

**What I checked:**  
I verified that all ten required named ranges were included in the workbook: `FC_AMT`, `S0_in`, `F0_in`, `R_USD`, `R_FC`, `K_PUT`, `K_CALL`, `PREM_PUT`, `PREM_CALL`, and `T_DAYS`. I also checked that calculated hedge outputs use formulas rather than manually entered result values.

**What I found:**  
All ten required named ranges were present and connected to the corresponding cells on the Inputs tab. The forward, money-market, option, validation, and sensitivity calculations were formula-driven.

**What I did:**  
No structural correction was necessary. I retained the named-range structure and confirmed that the workbook can be repopulated in Phase 4 by changing the input values without rewriting the hedge calculations.

## Finding 2 — Money-Market Hedge and Parity Check

**What I checked:**  
I reviewed the money-market hedge to confirm that it was displayed in the three explicit steps required by the specification: borrowing the present value of the EUR receivable, converting the EUR amount to USD at spot, and investing the USD until maturity. I also checked the covered interest parity calculation against the forward hedge.

**What I found:**  
During the build process, the initial illustrative interest-rate combination produced a larger difference between the CIP-implied forward and the scenario-provided indicative forward of 1.0890. This was caused by the placeholder assumptions rather than the hedge formulas themselves.

**What I did:**  
I revised the indicative Phase 3 interest-rate assumptions so that they were internally consistent with the scenario-provided forward while continuing to label them as placeholders. After the adjustment, the CIP-implied forward was approximately 1.08894 USD/EUR. The parity difference was approximately 0.0053%, and the workbook's parity check returned `PASS`. These rates will be replaced with live market rates in Phase 4.

## Finding 3 — Sensitivity and Option Calculations

**What I checked:**  
I verified that the sensitivity analysis covers ending EURUSD spot rates from 95% through 105% of `S0_in` in 1% increments. I also checked that the EUR put calculation behaves continuously around the strike and that the hedge strategies respond appropriately when the ending spot changes.

**What I found:**  
The sensitivity table contains exactly 11 scenarios, from -5% through +5%. The ending spot values and hedge proceeds are generated by formulas rather than manually entered values. The forward and money-market proceeds remain approximately constant across spot scenarios, while the unhedged and option strategies respond to changes in `S_T`. The put calculation also transitions correctly at the strike.

**What I did:**  
I retained the sensitivity formulas and comparison chart after confirming that the table recalculates from `S0_in`. I also kept the visible validation checks so that these requirements can be re-tested after live data are entered in Phase 4.

## Audit Conclusion

The workbook meets the Phase 2 specification and Phase 3 build contract. The audit confirmed that the required named ranges, formula-driven hedge calculations, three-step money-market hedge, options calculations, sensitivity analysis, and validation checks are present. The main issue identified during the build was inconsistency between the first set of illustrative interest-rate assumptions and the scenario-provided indicative forward. After correcting the placeholder assumptions, the parity check passed. The workbook is ready for Phase 4 live market-data population and cross-checking against the FIN 321 FX Hedging Lab.
